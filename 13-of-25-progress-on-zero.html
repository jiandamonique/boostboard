13 of 25: What Progress on Zero Looks Like

Volunteer Team · 2026-09-14

While we aim to focus on feelings not numbers, numbers can certainly help to quantify progress. We'll lean in. Where do we stand today? Let's have a look:

25
campaigns on the board
13
currently at zero donations
11
categories represented

The number that matters here is 13

Not 25. Thirteen of the twenty-five campaigns currently on the board — just over half — haven't received a single donation yet. Early on, finding even one genuine zero-donation campaign was the hardest single part of building this: plenty of real need exists, but a campaign that's truly, verifiably sitting at zero and willing to be featured is a narrower thing to find than it sounds. Now more than half the board qualifies. For a project built around that tier, that's not a stat to bury — it's the actual measure of whether this is working.

Where everything currently stands

Seed — 13 First Five — 7 First Ten — 4 Graduated — 1

One campaign has already graduated off the board entirely — passed ten donors and moved on, the way this is supposed to work. That's the part easy to lose sight of day to day: campaigns are supposed to leave. A crowded "graduated" list isn't a failure state, it's the whole point working as designed. And to be clear — graduating off the board doesn't mean the need is over. We encourage folks to keep donating, even past those small wins.

Growth wasn't a single push

The board didn't jump to 25 in one sitting. It built up in batches over about a week — a first wave of 15, then small additions of two, two, and six as real campaigns kept getting found and verified. Slower than a single mass import would have been, but every single one of these 25 is a real person's real campaign, checked individually rather than bulk-added. That's a deliberate tradeoff, not a limitation we're working around.

Eleven categories, not one story

Housing, medical costs, family support, financial hardship, education, animals, international aid, community projects, legal fees, sports, and crisis relief all currently have at least one campaign on the board. That range matters — "needing help" doesn't look like one specific situation, and a board that only reflected one kind of need would be quietly excluding a lot of real ones.

None of this changes what gets someone off zero: an actual donation, or an actual share. The count just tells us the sourcing is working. What happens next is still up to whoever's reading this.
