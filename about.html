Why this exists

About Boost Board

Picture a school bulletin board covered in thousands of drawings — but the light only shines on the ones that already have a gold star. If nobody's noticed a drawing yet, it stays in the dark corner.

Boost Board is like someone with a flashlight, pointing it at one hidden drawing every single day so people can finally see it.

  • The problem: big platforms tend to promote what's already popular. A brand-new fundraiser with no donations yet gets buried at the bottom.
  • The spotlight: real volunteers pick one overlooked campaign each day and put it front and center.
  • Fair turns: it changes daily, so more campaigns get a turn.
  • Hands-off: we never touch the money. When you give, it goes straight to the person who needs it.

1. Algorithms count people out

A fundraising page with zero donations tends to stay that way — not because the need isn't real, but because platforms surface what's already popular. Being first is the hardest part, and it has nothing to do with how much someone deserves help.

2. Predatory "boost your campaign" services

People trying to raise money for a real need are often approached by paid services promising visibility, virality, or guaranteed shares. At best these dilute the honesty of the original ask; at worst they take money from someone already struggling and leave them further discouraged when the promised results don't show up.

3. A story* about giving someone a good day

There's an old This American Life story (Episode 286, later re-aired as Episode 588, in a segment called "The Spy Who Loved Everyone") about a comedy group called Improv Everywhere. In 2004, they learned that a small unknown band from Vermont, Ghosts of Pasha, was about to play their first-ever New York show to what would likely be an almost empty room. So about three dozen volunteers learned the band's songs, made merchandise, and showed up acting like devoted, longtime superfans — mobbing the stage, singing along, treating total strangers like they'd just watched their favorite band on earth.

The intention was purely generous: give a band one incredible night. And for a while, it worked — the band fed off the energy and played like they meant it. But once they realized the crowd wasn't real, it left them unsettled rather than grateful. Manufactured encouragement, even offered with real love, can curdle once the truth comes out.

Here's the part that matters most to us, though: it didn't end there. Years later, Ghosts of Pasha came back and performed live at Improv Everywhere's own anniversary show — this time as themselves, on their own terms, seemingly at peace with the whole strange story. The good intention was real. It just needed honesty to actually land.

Our goal isn't a sea of $0 campaign pages

We're not trying to advertise or encourage zero-donation fundraisers as some kind of category to collect. If you look at our board and every campaign already has a donor or two, that's not a failure of this project — it might be the opposite.

Sharing a campaign, submitting it to us, even just someone talking about it — any of that activity can be exactly the kind of signal a fundraising platform's own algorithm notices. By the time a campaign reaches us, or by the time we get around to featuring it, it may already have picked up its first supporter. That's not us missing the moment. That's the actual goal arriving a little early.

Our real goal isn't a board full of zero-dollar campaigns to point at. It's the opposite — we'd love to see no zero-dollar fundraisers at all. Either way this plays out, it's a win.

"Zero" here is really shorthand for low activity, not always a literal $0. You might send us a campaign through our contact form, and by the time it's actually posted, it may no longer be at zero. We consider that a good thing. <3

A personal note

This project also has a more personal thread. A few of our volunteers have been going through hard times of our own. We talked it through together, in a group chat, and decided to try turning that into something useful instead of just something hard. We're choosing to stay hopeful about where this goes.
