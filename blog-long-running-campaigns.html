If a Campaign Is Still Up, It's Still Worth Donating To

Boost Board · 2026-10-13

One of the most common reasons people skip a campaign: it looks old. The date says it launched a year ago — sometimes two. The raised amount hasn't moved in a while. It feels like you missed something.

You didn't.

Old isn't the same as over

GoFundMe campaigns stay live until the organizer closes them. If a campaign is still up, the organizer still wants donations. That's not an oversight on their part — it's an open door. A campaign that hasn't reached its goal in 18 months is a campaign that still hasn't reached its goal. The need didn't expire. The person is still there.

Some campaigns are built to outlast the moment

A lot of campaigns are tied to a specific event — surgery recovery, a move, a car repair — but the need they describe is ongoing. Medical debt doesn't go away the week after surgery. A car still isn't replaced after the immediate crisis fades from view. These campaigns see a spike of donations at launch, then go quiet, then the person is left managing the same gap for months or years.

Other campaigns are explicitly long-haul: ongoing caregiving, chronic illness, a small business rebuilding slowly. There's no target date. There's just the work of getting through.

Timing and ratios don't tell the whole story

A campaign at 4% funded after two years and a campaign at 4% funded after two weeks look identical in the data. They're not the same situation. Sometimes a campaign moves slowly because the organizer doesn't have a large social network — not because the need is any less real.

If a campaign is still up and the link works and the story checks out, a donation is warranted. The age of the campaign is not a reason to pass. It might be the best reason to stop and give.
