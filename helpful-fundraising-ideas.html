Helpful Fundraising Ideas

A few things that help, no matter which platform you're on

Boost Board exists precisely because doing everything "right" doesn't always work — algorithms bury good campaigns for reasons that have nothing to do with how hard you tried or how much your need is real. Nothing below is a requirement, and if none of it changes your reach, that's not a reflection on you. These are just things that can help alongside the board, not instead of it.

It's okay to ask someone close to give first

  • A page with one donation on it reads differently to a stranger than an empty one — that's just how people are wired, not a judgment on your campaign.
  • If you're comfortable, asking one trusted person — even for a small amount — to be first isn't "gaming" anything. It's a normal, low-pressure way to get started.
  • If that's not something you have access to, or it doesn't feel right for your situation, that's completely fine too — it's one option, not a prerequisite.

Update your page, even in small ways

  • A short new sentence or a new photo brings your page back to the top of people's feeds.
  • You don't need big news to post an update — "still working on this, thank you for reading" is enough.

A personal message often lands better than a public post

  • A direct text or call to one or two people usually does more than one public post to everyone at once.
  • It's okay to ask more than once. People forget, not ignore.

Be specific, if you're able to

  • A clear number and a clear reason ("$400 for a car repair") is easier for people to act on than a general appeal.
  • One clear photo helps more than several unclear ones.

A short update helps, even during a wait

  • If things are quiet for a while, a brief note — even just "still waiting, thank you for your patience" — helps people feel connected instead of forgotten.
  • This is optional, not an obligation. Waiting in silence isn't a mistake either.

Say thank you publicly

  • Thanking people who've already given — even a quick line — encourages others watching to do the same.

Some donors prefer to stay anonymous — that's okay

  • Most donation platforms let a giver choose to stay anonymous. Respecting that means not trying to guess or reveal who they are, even privately.
  • Many platforms also let you set up an automatic thank-you message that goes out to every donor the moment they give — this works well specifically because it doesn't require knowing who someone is, so it reaches anonymous givers just as warmly as anyone else.
  • A general public thank-you — "grateful to everyone who's given, including those who chose to stay anonymous" — lets you express real appreciation without singling anyone out.

Try more than one place

  • The same link can go to community groups, local mutual aid networks, or spaces specific to your situation — not just a single platform's built-in audience.
  • Even with all the best practices, sometimes there just is not capacity to do anything more than post your page or have someone or a community post it for you. It's hard enough to ask for help and we are all doing what we can as we can. We are all rooting for you.
